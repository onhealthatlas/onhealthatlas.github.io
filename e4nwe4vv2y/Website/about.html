Why we built this

Governments collect a great deal of information about housing and health care. Most of it ends up in spreadsheets and technical reports that few people ever read, and it rarely shows what’s happening in a particular town or neighbourhood.

Since 2018, Doug Ford’s Progressive Conservative government has been responsible for Ontario’s housing policy and its health care system. Promises are easy to make. These maps show what has actually happened: what rent costs, how many homes are being built, and whether people can find a family doctor, in the places where Ontarians live.

We believe everyone in Ontario should be able to hold their government to account with facts. Ontario Insights puts those facts in one place, in a form anyone can use.

What you’ll find

  • Health care: how many family doctors practise in each community and how many people have one.
  • Affordability: core housing need, mortgage payments, and the price of new homes.
  • Renting: rents, vacancy and turnover, from big cities to small towns.
  • Home building: how many homes are being started and finished, and what kind.
  • Community and seniors’ housing: supply, cost, condition and ownership.

New maps are added as data is released, including more health care maps and a census data explorer. See all maps.

About Dr. Adil Shamji

Dr. Adil Shamji is the Member of Provincial Parliament (MPP) for Don Valley East in Toronto. He was first elected in 2022. He is also a physician.

In the legislature, he serves as the Ontario Liberal critic for:

  • HousingRents, home building, affordability and homelessness.
  • Indigenous AffairsThe province’s relationships with, and obligations to, First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities.
  • Primary Care, Urgent Care and Public HealthAccess to family doctors, urgent and emergency care, and the public health system.

Learn more about Dr. Shamji at adilshamji.ca

What a critic does

At Queen’s Park, opposition parties assign MPPs to follow specific areas of government. These are called critic roles. A critic questions the minister responsible, examines new laws and spending, and proposes alternatives.

Ontario Insights is part of that work. It gathers the evidence behind the questions, and shares it with the people most affected.

How the maps are made

Every map uses publicly available data from sources such as Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation, Statistics Canada and provincial health data. We don’t change the numbers. We put them on a map and explain what they mean.

Each map page lists its source, the type of area it covers and the time period. For definitions and tips, see How to use the maps.
