The maps

15 maps on housing and health care, with more on the way. Choose a map to open it, or narrow the list by topic.

Best on a computer or tabletMost maps are detailed and need a bigger screen. You can browse and open them on a phone, but some controls may be hard to use.

Health care

Millions of Ontarians don’t have a family doctor. See where the gaps are, and whether they’re closing.

  • Family doctors and their patients How many family doctors practise in each community, and how many patients are formally enrolled with them. By municipality and riding, January 2026
  • Who has a family doctor The share of residents with a regular family doctor or primary care team, and how it changed from 2020 to 2022. By postal area and riding, 2020 and 2022

Affordability

What it costs to keep a roof overhead, whether you rent or own, and how many households can’t manage it.

  • Households in core housing need The share of households whose home is unaffordable, overcrowded or needs major repairs, with no affordable alternative nearby. By metro area, 2018 to 2024
  • Mortgage payments and loan sizes The average monthly mortgage payment and the average size of new mortgages, every three months since 2012. By metro area, 2012 to 2025, quarterly
  • New home prices Average prices of newly built single-detached, semi-detached and row homes. Municipalities over 50,000 people, 2023
  • New homes sold, by price How many newly built homes sold in each price range, broken down by type of home. Urban areas over 50,000 people, January to June 2026

Renting

What renters pay and how hard it is to find an apartment, from Toronto to towns of a few thousand people.

  • Rent and vacancy since 2006 Average apartment rent and the share of apartments empty and available to rent, year by year. By city and town, 2006 to 2023
  • Two-bedroom rents and turnover What a two-bedroom apartment costs, how many are empty, and how often tenants move out. By metro area, 2024 and 2025
  • Rents in larger communities Average apartment rent, number of rental apartments and vacancy rate in every municipality with more than 10,000 people. Municipalities over 10,000 people, 2023
  • Rents in small towns Average apartment rent, number of rental apartments and vacancy rate in communities of 2,500 to 10,000 people. Municipalities of 2,500 to 10,000 people, 2025
  • Rent by number of bedrooms Median rent for bachelor, one-, two- and three-bedroom apartments, neighbourhood by neighbourhood in Ontario’s cities. By neighbourhood zone in major cities, 2025

Home building

The province promised 1.5 million new homes by 2031. See how many are actually being built, and what kind.

  • Homes started, finished and under construction How many new homes broke ground, how many were completed, and how many are still being built. Municipalities over 50,000 people, 2026
  • What kinds of homes are being built New home construction split into detached houses, semis, row houses, apartments and other types. Municipalities over 10,000 people, 2026

Community and seniors’ housing

Homes meant to stay within reach: how many there are, what shape they’re in, and who runs them.

  • Community and affordable housing Where community housing is, what it costs to rent, what condition it’s in, and who owns and runs it. By municipality, July 2026
  • Seniors’ housing How many standard spaces there are in seniors’ residences, and how many sit empty. By metro area, 2010 to 2021

Why this site exists

Governments publish a great deal of information about housing and health care, but most of it sits in spreadsheets and technical reports that few people ever open. Ontario Insights puts that information on maps, so anyone can look up their own community.

Doug Ford’s Progressive Conservatives have governed Ontario since 2018. Housing and health care are provincial responsibilities, and the results of their decisions show up in these numbers. You should be able to see them for yourself.
